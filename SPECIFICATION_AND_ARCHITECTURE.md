### Acceptance criteria for approval and the pilot

1. A supported lab result remains unavailable to the patient and proxy until an authorized clinician separately releases it. An urgent or unclassified result stays held until the required review and, where urgent, a recorded completion of the critical process. Lab signoff alone never releases it.
2. The patient can see only their own permitted results; an individually permitted caregiver sees only their permitted view; a clinician must have a recorded encounter with that patient. Staff can inspect the permitted access log and cannot inspect clinical results through a staff role. Once removal of a caregiver permission is saved, new requests are denied.
3. Tests below and at ages 12 and 18 show all three protected encounter categories and their results hidden from proxies from 12 onward, with all proxy access ending at 18. A hidden item and an item that never existed produce the same public response; pages, totals and trends disclose no hidden-record count or activity. Timing is investigated rather than asserted safe without evidence.
4. Each permitted clinical view is saved together with its access record before any results are shown; denied attempts are recorded while the database is available. If saving the required access record fails, no clinical content is shown. A complete database outage is reported safely, with an honest statement of what could not be recorded.
5. Equivalent creatinine measurements, used to estimate kidney function, agree after converting either specified unit; the two blood-pressure numbers are interpreted independently; the supplied ranges use age and clinical sex at the measurement time. Missing or unsuitable inputs give a clear cannot-compute reason; the “no applicable range” state looks different from “normal.”
6. Repeating the same accepted submission with the same submission reference creates no duplicate clinical records. A submission with an invalid entry identifies it and saves none of that submission's clinical records. Corrections retain the original and both versions remain retrievable by an entitled reader without revealing confidential versions to others.
7. Patients can inspect the results list, accessible trend chart and permitted access log using a keyboard; flags are understandable without color. The display identifies correction links and the dates of inputs used for derived values.
8. The real source format and correction/unit behavior are verified before the connector is approved. No identifiable outbound disclosure occurs without both required recorded permissions; no insurer export is part of this release.
9. Before pilot expansion, the team supplies a successful restore record, agreed read-response targets with representative measurements, reconciled source/destination counts, and approval from the named clinical, privacy/security and operational owners. Assessment performance and restore evidence must state their actual dataset and conditions; they do not prove readiness at all-clinic scale.

Use the supplied five tables and preserve all 27 source column meanings. Keep the explicit primary keys, `encounter.patient_id` and `observation.patient_id` foreign keys, the proxy `(patient_id,proxy_id)` primary-key pair, and the four expressly nullable Observation fields (`encounter_id`, `value_num`, `correction_of`, JSONB `component`). Additional NOT NULL, foreign-key, check, uniqueness and privilege constraints must be identified as our decisions in migration notes; do not attribute them to the brief. Use relational columns for patient/time/code/encounter/status/canonical numeric values, and JSONB for the defined component structure rather than an opaque JSON document for every query.

V1–V3 implement the source model plus `actor`, `patient_sex_context`, immutable `ingestion_receipt` records, separate mutable `observation_review` metadata, revocation attribution and minimally categorical audit provenance. Preserve `patient.sex` as the current explicit recorded clinical-sex assertion, with observation-time history/context retained separately. A nullable numeric value supports component observations and unavailable quantities; it never means fabricated zero. Runtime can read/append the permitted clinical/audit data and narrowly update review/proxy metadata, but cannot UPDATE/DELETE source Observations or audit events, provision actors, create schema or act as a superuser. The short-lived migration owner is excluded from serving and runtime workflow contexts. These additions and extra FKs/checks are implementation choices documented in [schema and privilege notes](docs/DATABASE.md); they do not alter the 27 supplied column meanings.

At roughly 9 million observations across 175,000 patients, with about 3 million observations added each year, start with a single well-indexed relational primary and tested backup/recovery rather than sharding by assumption. Store canonical quantities with sufficient decimal precision, UTC instants for observation/access events and complete dates for verified dates of birth; retain unsupported or partial demographic context explicitly as unavailable, never silently synthesize a full date. The specification's numbers do not determine disk usage: actual row/component widths, retained versions, index size, audit rate, WAL, backups and retention must be measured. In particular, reads generate audit writes, so clinical data growth alone does not size the database.

These index definitions were inspected in the current migrations; a definition is not measured query-plan or scale evidence. PostgreSQL multicolumn B-tree behavior supports equality filters before range/order columns, but actual authorized query plans and rows read/returned must be measured on the stated dataset. Partial read indexes must match real predicates; do not assume an arbitrary parameterized predicate implies a partial-index predicate. [PostgreSQL 16 multicolumn indexes](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/16/indexes-multicolumn.html), [partial indexes](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/16/indexes-partial.html). Exact submicrosecond residuals preserve supported nanoseconds rather than rounding across an interpretation date boundary.

Do not serve patient result bodies from a shared cache or stale replica: current entitlement/release filtering, revocation and same-transaction audit need one consistent primary action. The list/detail implementation first loads the eligible patient history, filters complete visible correction ancestry, derives from that view, then counts and pages. This has per-patient Java memory/CPU cost; a SQL index or 15-second transaction timeout does not hard-bound arbitrary Java computation. Avoid silently truncating history or ancestry to improve a benchmark. Result paging keeps the required offset/limit contract, with adopted offset 0–1,000,000 and limit 1–200; a bound is not proof deep offsets are cheap. Proposed larger-scale projections, streaming or partitioning must be measured and preserve the same complete visibility, correction and audit semantics before adoption.

### Expected first tenfold-scale bottleneck and detection

For planning, tenfold record volume means approximately 90 million observations across 1.75 million patients, with about 30 million added annually if the stated growth also scales tenfold. That arithmetic is a scenario, not a load test; the brief does not specify concurrent users, observations per patient, component widths or read-to-write ratio. My first expected limit under a proportionately busier workload is the primary database's request work: exact visible counts and long histories contend with synchronous audit inserts, WAL/commit flush and the connection pool. Selectivity loss, deep offsets, long trend/derived-input scans and correction traversal can make individual patients expensive even when the whole table is indexed. This is a forecast, not a proven bottleneck; if traffic remains unchanged, storage, backup/restore duration or maintenance may be the first operational limit instead.

Measure per-route p50/p95/p99 latency and errors under representative role/visibility cases, DB pool wait and transaction duration, rows read versus returned, slow query plans and buffer/disk reads, lock waits, commit/WAL latency, audit insert volume, database/index growth, dead tuples/autovacuum and backup/restore duration. Track ingestion lag and failed/retried submissions separately. Compare those signals with the declared endpoint target at a named dataset size; no production response time is asserted here. First improve the demonstrated hot query or index, offer client-requested bounded chart windows without silently truncating requested trends, and shorten transaction duration. Do not remove required audit or hide total-count errors to make a benchmark pass. Consider partitioning/archive or purpose-built internal projections only after measurements establish the access pattern, with correction integrity, retention and cross-partition lookups explicitly preserved. Switching this assessment contract to cursor paging would be a future API decision, not a silent optimization.

### Ingestion, replay and retained amendments

The implemented adapter accepts JSON FHIR R4 transaction Bundles with POST creates of Patient, Encounter and Observation. This candidate-adopted subset rejects unsupported resources/interactions/clinical representations atomically; it is not general FHIR-server support. Bounded UTF-8 transport and strict JSON parsing precede expensive work: maximum 2 MiB and 1,000 entries, with further nesting/string/numeric bounds in the [ingestion contract](docs/FHIR_INGESTION.md). The complete fullUrl map permits forward/backward `urn:uuid:` references; relative stored references undergo type, existence and entitlement checks without remote fetching. Server-assigned IDs, not supplied IDs, identify new resources. Incoming source assertions cannot create trusted clinician/proxy rights.

Pure HAPI structural/invariant validation and strict parsing use only submitted bytes and bundled definitions before any database transaction. Preparation has a cooperative 45-second budget checked between/after entries; it cannot interrupt a validator call already running and is not production admission control. All stored roster/receipt/authorization/reference reads, clinical normalization/planning, inserts, audit and completed outcome still share one fresh 15-second read-write Repeatable Read action. Prepared checks are reused across whole-action database retries. The action rechecks the actor and durable receipt before considering prepared failures: an exact completed receipt can replay during validator unavailability; a new request gets audited 503 without a completed receipt; an unknown actor retains 404 without exposing validator status. Invalid header/transport requests skip preparation. The database timeout was not increased and the Bundle was not split into partial clinical commits.

**Declared FHIR discrepancy:** the brief requests invalid-entry naming in a transaction-response, while normative R4 specifies an OperationOutcome for a failed transaction. The adopted assessment-first envelope is HTTP 400 with a transaction-response preserving request order, safe indexed outcomes for invalid entries and uncommitted siblings. No clinical writes survive rejection. Malformed JSON, wrong Bundle type or transport/header failures use a safe top-level OperationOutcome; infrastructure failure is safe 5xx and key/body conflict 409. This deliberately differs from the normative failed-transaction envelope; do not claim full FHIR REST conformance. [FHIR R4 transaction response](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/http.html#transaction-response)

Require a 1–128 character `[A-Za-z0-9._-]` idempotency key bound to checked actor ID/role and SHA-256 of exact received UTF-8 bytes. A same-scope/key/body completed request replays saved status/body/IDs after actor revalidation; changed bytes, including whitespace, give 409. Durable success or deterministic validation failures remain for the assessment database lifetime; temporary infrastructure failures remain retryable. Database coordination and uniqueness prevent duplicate accepted effects. SQLSTATE 40001/40P01 retries the entire action in a fresh snapshot, at most three attempts, not a statement inside an aborted transaction.

A correction appends a fresh source linked by `correction_of`; it never overwrites the original or misuses `derivedFrom`. Same-patient/analyte head target, unchanged encounter/effective context, complete context checks and one-successor uniqueness reject cycles, branching or unsupported context-changing corrections. Appending coordinates on the predecessor's separate mutable review row with first release. A stale overlapping updater retries the whole action and sees the winning state; a held predecessor superseded first cannot newly release, while an already validly released original stays retained. Never release a known-wrong original to enable a correction's ancestry.
