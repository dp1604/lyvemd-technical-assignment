# Group 1 — proposed access, clinical and audit policies B v1

Status: PROPOSED by Codex; not adopted yet. Covers Tasks 14–17 and the remaining time/clinical details in Task 11. Package A v1 was adopted separately. These are explicit assessment policies, not invented source facts, production identity assurance or clinical sign-off.

## B1. Test identity, reads and access-log rights

Require exactly one bounded actor ID and one exact role header. Actor IDs are 1–64 ASCII letters/digits/dot/underscore/hyphen; roles are PATIENT, PROXY, CLINICIAN or STAFF. Check the declared pair against a seeded authoritative test roster, then check stored patient/encounter/proxy relationships. No real authentication or identity provisioning is added. These headers remain impersonatable test declarations, even with roster validation.

Clinical reads: PATIENT is the patient themself; PROXY requires an active individual link; CLINICIAN must be on at least one encounter for that patient; STAFF has no clinical-result access. Patient and proxy views also obey release/critical holds; proxy views additionally obey confidentiality/expiry. Clinicians can review held results only for patients to whom their encounter entitlement applies.

Recommend access-log rights for STAFF and the patient themself only. Proxy and clinician log requests are denied, preventing that route from exposing confidential or held-result activity. This extra role policy is not specified by the brief. Log responses contain required audit fields but no result values, analytes or detailed clinical query parameters in action/source. New requests still record attributable, minimally necessary events.

Only a known test CLINICIAN may ingest Bundles. This is a local write-permission choice, not a new brief role or proof of production integration security. Malformed/missing/duplicate headers return a patient-existence-independent 400; a well-formed but unknown/mismatched actor or unauthorized patient lookup follows the same safe 404 behavior as an unavailable patient. Audit the attempted action where the database is available; missing/invalid identity is marked unverified, never assigned to a fabricated person.

## B2. Confidentiality calendar and endpoint details

Use the server request's UTC calendar date for proxy age: before 12 ordinary proxy rules apply; at 12, ABSL/SUBU/MHEV encounters and their observations, including historical ones, are hidden; at 18 all proxy access ends. Unknown/inconsistent birth date fails closed for proxy access. UTC is a reproducible demonstration convention, not an inferred Aldercrest location or legal rule. Clinical ranges/calculations instead use completed calendar age on the observation's effective UTC date.

For paging choose offset=0 and limit=50 by default; accept offset 0–1,000,000 and limit 1–200, rejecting malformed/out-of-range values. Results sort by effective descending then ID; audit rows by at descending then ID. Trends sort by effective ascending then ID, with an exact analyte filter. Date-only from/to cover those complete UTC dates; timestamps must have an offset and are inclusive bounds. Reject reversed bounds. An omitted bound is open-ended; do not fabricate dates.

A readable patient with no visible matches gets an empty page and visible-only total. A hidden or nonexistent detail gets the same 404 status/body and equivalent processing path; investigate timing differences honestly. No universal timing-indistinguishability claim is inferred from a few tests. Reject a request above 2 MiB or 1,000 Bundle entries before expensive processing, with a safe error. These limits are our stated bounds, not supplied numbers.

Additional correction-profile safeguard: require the original's encounter and effective time to remain the same; context-changing corrections are unsupported and rejected, not silently moved out of protection. Determine latest versions and supersession metadata within the caller's visible records; a concealed successor cannot remove or relabel a visible original. A correction is withheld if an ancestor is concealed, preserving correctionOf without exposing a hidden ancestor. This can hold a correction whose original was never released; never force release of a known-wrong original to enable its correction. Entitled clinicians can review the retained chain. These are explicit assessment-profile limits, not normative FHIR requirements.

## B3. Historical clinical sex and demographics

Recommend explicit effective-dated synthetic clinical-sex assertions, rather than silently substituting Patient.gender. FHIR defines that field as administrative gender and warns it may differ from the aspect needed by decision support. [FHIR R4 Patient.gender](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/patient-definitions.html#Patient.gender)

Use a documented project-defined Patient extension `urn:atlas:StructureDefinition:clinical-sex-context`, with children `code` (valueCode male/female/unknown), `validFrom` (valueDate) and optional `validToExclusive` (valueDate). Intervals use the UTC calendar and [from,to) semantics. Include its StructureDefinition. Reject overlapping/inconsistent assertions. Add history storage while preserving patient.sex as the current recorded clinical-sex value; unknown history stays unknown. The synthetic generator explicitly supplies the needed intervals; no assertion is inferred from an administrative field or from the date the data was imported.

At ingestion, record the observation's applicable clinical-sex/birth-date context and provenance. Missing/partial/invalid age context or missing/conflicting historical sex produces an unavailable age/sex-dependent range or derived result with a reason; unaffected age-independent ranges still apply. Do not overwrite old interpretation silently after a demographic change. Package A's create-only surface does not implement general demographic editing; that limitation must be stated.

Alternative, if requested before adoption: a clearly declared synthetic-only assertion that clinical sex is stable for the entire generated history. I recommend the explicit effective-dated approach above because it exercises historical interpretation without pretending that stability is a real patient fact.

## B4. Derived values, units and numerical boundaries

Use inclusive normal-range endpoints: LOW below the lower bound, HIGH above the upper bound, NORMAL within both; NO_APPLICABLE_FLAG and its reason remain distinct. Do not invent age restrictions absent from the table. Non-finite/malformed numbers or unsupported unit representations fail validation. Missing or physiologically unusable calculation inputs produce an explicit cannot-compute reason, never a fabricated value; a validly represented source measurement need not be silently erased because a derivation is unavailable.

Either a weight or height event can form a BMI derivation anchor. Apply the caller's authorization/release/confidentiality filter first, then choose the latest eligible visible weight and height at or before that anchor; no future measurement is used. Missing and concealed height share the identical public HEIGHT_UNAVAILABLE shape/reason. Invalid or ambiguous visible inputs also produce explicit safe cannot-compute reasons. Preserve the chosen source IDs/times. A new concealed height must not change a calculation based on older visible inputs.

Call this the latest-input calculation available to the caller, not the globally latest or clinically current BMI. It can differ from a clinician's fuller view; show source measurement dates and a view-scoped interpretation notice. No approved staleness window was supplied, so do not invent one or claim clinical freshness. The production clinical owner must validate that interpretation. This is our explicit privacy-preserving interpretation of “most recent” within an authorized view, not a wording supplied by the brief.

Derived output inherits the intersection of its selected visible inputs' restrictions and never incorporates a concealed/unreleased measurement. Use latest-visible correction heads for view-scoped derived series; retain authorized raw original/corrected retrieval. Only final/amended/corrected observations are eligible for release and derived calculations; other valid statuses remain labelled source records for clinician review, not silently finalized. Test missing versus concealed inputs and absent versus concealed newer versions/measurements, including totals and metadata.

Use the published race-free 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine equation only for age 18+, known applicable clinical sex and valid positive standardized creatinine. Normalize umol/L to mg/dL by dividing by 88.4, calculate in mL/min/1.73m2, then divide by exactly 60 for the required per-second value. Preserve equation/version and input provenance. Under-18, missing/unsupported or inconsistent inputs yield a reason; no unsupported upper-age cutoff or laboratory assay interval is invented. The documented synthetic feed profile explicitly asserts creatinine standardization; LOINC 2160-0/Quantity alone is not proof of assay traceability. No real assay has been verified; actual standardization and measuring-interval verification remain upstream production gates. [NIDDK adult equation/conversion](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/research-funding/research-programs/kidney-clinical-research-epidemiology/laboratory/glomerular-filtration-rate-equations/adults)

Choose eGFR LOINC 98979-8. For BP's parent Observation choose 85354-9 and require the two task-specified components 8480-6/8462-4; the parent code is our choice, not supplied by the task. Verify numerical results against independent NKF examples in Group 3. [LOINC eGFR](https://loinc.org/98979-8), [LOINC BP panel](https://loinc.org/85354-9)

UCUM clarification: the micro prefix is `u`; µmol/L may be a display label, while the supported creatinine machine code is `umol/L` with the UCUM system. The task's eGFR label `mL/s/1.73m2` is preserved in display/response unit text, while the machine unitCode/unit_code uses `mL/s/{1.73_m2}`. Do not parse the printed decimal label as a UCUM expression: a period is multiplication in UCUM, and LOINC uses the normalized-body-area annotation form. Record this source-notation clarification in the specification; physical units and the task's per-second output stay unchanged. Other analytes accept their listed canonical codes. [UCUM grammar/prefixes](https://ucum.org/ucum), [LOINC example unit](https://loinc.org/98979-8#example-units)

## B5. Clinician release and critical handling

Every new/amended result begins HELD. Observation.status=final alone never releases it. Use a local clinician review/release command through the same service authorization and audit logic, requiring a known CLINICIAN with stored encounter entitlement. No new public release endpoint, login or notification service is added.

Criticality begins UNKNOWN unless an upstream critical interpretation is explicitly supplied. Preserve HH/LL/AA critical signals from the observation or its components; calculated HIGH/LOW flags do not become critical thresholds. Unknown cases stay held until explicit clinician classification. A NONCRITICAL review can proceed to a separate release action. A CRITICAL result additionally requires recorded critical-path-completion attestation before a separate release. Record clinician, time, classification, attestation and action audit. Do not downgrade a supplied critical signal through an ordinary noncritical classification. [FHIR interpretation codes](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/v3/ObservationInterpretation/cs.html)

The generator invokes the actual review/release actions for synthetic examples rather than directly inserting released=true. The critical-path record is a demonstration attestation, not proof that a real patient was contacted or that a complete production clinical workflow exists. The note must assign the real clinical workflow/threshold/communication ownership and verification gate; no thresholds are invented and notifications remain excluded.

## B6. Read/audit transaction and failure policy

Interpret “own transaction” as one transaction for each content-read request/action, containing BOTH authorized/filtered reads and the audit row. This directly preserves R5's same-transaction requirement. Fully materialize results and commit before sending clinical content. Include internal clinical-content reads during ingestion validation and local clinician actions in their action transaction, auditing each affected patient. Also audit all four GET endpoints, including access-log reads, without recursive self-auditing.

Expected denials are returned outcomes inside the transaction: insert the denied event and commit, then map to the safe response. Do not lose the denial through a thrown rollback exception. Use a consistent Repeatable Read request snapshot for entitlement, page/count and clinical context. Revocation committed before that snapshot denies the request; a revocation after an already-authorized snapshot cannot retroactively retract its in-flight response. Bound request duration and document that concurrency boundary. [PostgreSQL 16 transactions](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/16/tutorial-transactions.html), [isolation](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/16/transaction-iso.html)

If required audit persistence/commit fails, withhold clinical content and return a safe unavailable response. Database unavailability makes writing a denial to that same database impossible: disclose that limitation and emit a non-clinical operational signal, without claiming guaranteed denied-event durability during total database failure. Do not substitute an asynchronous or separate audit transaction for R5's successful-read guarantee.
